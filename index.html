THE PROBLEM

Critical events can happen between manual inspections.

Limited visibility

Continuous observation of large flocks by farm workers is impractical.

Delayed detection

Mortality and abnormal inactivity may remain unnoticed until the next inspection round.

Operational impact

Delayed response can contribute to avoidable losses, biosecurity concerns, and animal-welfare risks.

DETECTION SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

A suspected mortality alert prompts inspection.

What it detects

A single-class “Dead Bird” object detector flags visual patterns consistent with dead birds. It does not predict future mortality or diagnose disease.

Known failure modes

Resting or sleeping live birds can trigger false positives. Low light, distance and occlusion can cause missed detections. Reliable separation of resting and dead birds is not yet established.

Human confirmation

A farm worker checks each suspected event and confirms the bird's condition before action. Temporal cues and explicit live/resting classes are planned research improvements.

Planned pilot measurements: confirmed-event recall, false alerts per house per day, time to notification and inspection, uptime, and operator feedback. Acceptance targets will be agreed with the pilot farm before testing.
